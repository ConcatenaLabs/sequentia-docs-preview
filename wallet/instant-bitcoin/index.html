Instant bitcoin: Ark and Lightning

Live on testnet For users

Send bitcoin in seconds, for free, without opening a channel, without worrying about liquidity, and without ever giving up your keys.

What went wrong with Lightning for ordinary people

Lightning works. It is fast, cheap and trust-minimised. But it asks its users to do a job: open channels, keep them funded, and make sure there is enough capacity pointing in the right direction before anyone can pay them. Most people do not want that job, so most Lightning wallets quietly became custodial.

The Sequentia wallet takes a different route. It uses two technologies together, each for what it is best at.

Ark for you, Lightning for the rest of the world

Ark. When bitcoin arrives in your wallet, the wallet moves it into a shared Bitcoin output that holds the coins of many users at once, called a batch. Inside it, each user holds their own coin, and users pay each other instantly and for free. There are no channels and no question of who has liquidity towards whom: everyone can send and receive straight away.

Lightning. To pay someone outside the ark, or be paid by them, the payment travels over the Lightning Network, to another ark or to any Lightning node. Lightning becomes plumbing between professional operators, which is the job it is good at. You never see a channel.

The Bitcoin blockchain. For larger amounts, the wallet waits for the next Bitcoin block, about ten minutes. The limit is the ark’s own, and the wallet reads it from the ark’s server.

Another wallet on the same ark Inside the ark Seconds Nothing

It is real bitcoin

This is not a pegged coin and not an IOU. There is no peg-in, no peg-out and no bitcoin derivative.

The coin you hold in an ark is yours in the strongest sense: you hold a transaction, signed in advance, that takes your coin out to the Bitcoin blockchain without anyone’s cooperation. If the ark’s server disappears or refuses to serve you, you leave on your own. The wallet does this by itself when it has to.

Go deeperWhat the server can and cannot do

The ark’s server co-signs payments and builds the shared outputs. It cannot spend your coin: every payment needs your signature, and your exit to the chain needs only yours.

What the server controls is convenience. Each shared output expires after a fixed period, so coins are renewed into a new one before then. The web wallet schedules each renewal with the server whenever the page is open, and the server then performs it shortly before expiry even with the page closed. If the server refuses the wallet or stops answering and a coin worth its exit is within a day of expiry, the wallet takes it to the chain without the server.

An exit costs on-chain fees, so the wallet needs a little confirmed bitcoin at its own address to pay for one, and a coin worth less than its exit cannot sensibly be exited.

The Bitcoin ark is Bark, developed by Second. The testnet runs its own Bark server on Bitcoin testnet4. Details: the web wallet README.

Go deeperWhat covenants would change

Ark works on Bitcoin today. It would work better with covenants: a small consensus upgrade that lets an output restrict how it can be spent. Proposals include BIP119 (CTV), TXHASH and BIP348 (CSFS).

On Sequentia covenants already exist, inherited from Elements, and Arca, the Ark protocol for Sequentia assets, is built on them. A holder schedules a renewal once and the operator runs it without the holder being online, and a holder who stays away does not lose the coin.

Getting started

There is no setup. Either import an existing Bitcoin seed, or create a new wallet and send it some bitcoin. The wallet moves the funds into the ark by itself.

On the testnet, the faucet button for BTC pays your wallet directly on the ark, in seconds. Open the web wallet and try it.

Where it stands

The wallet has run on Bitcoin testnet4 since July 2026. The code is public, and a launch of the wallet on Bitcoin mainnet is planned for 2027. Until then, use the testnet: it costs nothing and nothing can be lost.
