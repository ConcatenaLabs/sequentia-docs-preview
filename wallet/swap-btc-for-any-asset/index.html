Swap BTC for any asset

Live on testnet For users Simplifies the whitepaper, section i and Principle 5

The bitcoin stays on Bitcoin. The asset stays on Sequentia. The trade binds them together, and nobody holds either one in the middle.

The problem with wrapped bitcoin

Every other chain that wants to trade against bitcoin has the same problem: bitcoin is not there. So somebody locks real bitcoin away and issues a token that represents it. That token is only as good as whoever holds the keys.

  • WBTC, the largest wrapped bitcoin, changed its custody arrangement in 2024. The change was contested, and major lending protocols paused or cut their exposure to it.
  • Liquid requires real BTC to be locked with a federation. In September 2026 a software defect let an attacker create about 4,000 unbacked L-BTC and redeem them for real bitcoin: roughly 95% of the reserve. Most was returned, some 598 BTC was not, and withdrawals froze for weeks.

Sequentia’s answer is to remove the wrapped coin entirely. There is no pegged bitcoin anywhere in its design. Had the same bug been exploited on Sequentia, nobody would have lost a bitcoin, because nobody had given one to anyone else.

How a swap works, in plain words

You want to sell 100 dollars of a stablecoin for bitcoin. Someone else wants the opposite.

  1. Your stablecoins are locked on Sequentia. They can go to the buyer only with proof that the bitcoin was paid on Bitcoin.
  2. The buyer pays the bitcoin on Bitcoin: an ordinary payment, from any Bitcoin wallet.
  3. Once the payment is in a Bitcoin block, the buyer claims your stablecoins by proving it. If the buyer never pays, your stablecoins come back to you after a timeout.

No exchange holds the funds at any point. No account, no deposit, no withdrawal.

Three ways a trade settles

The wallet chooses the route and shows you, before you sign, exactly what you give, what you get and what you pay.

RouteBetweenSpeedState
On-chain, same chainTwo Sequentia assetsAbout a minuteLive
On-chain, cross-chainA Sequentia asset and BTC on BitcoinWaits for Bitcoin blocksLive
LightningA Sequentia asset and BTC, both over LightningSecondsExperimental

On the testnet, a trade against on-chain bitcoin can take an hour or more, because Bitcoin testnet4 blocks are irregular. The wallet’s Your trades list shows each step as it completes.

Why anchoring makes this possible

Between two unrelated chains, a swap must wait a long time on both, because either chain could reorganise on its own and undo one leg while the other stands.

Sequentia is anchored to Bitcoin: every block names a Bitcoin block, and if Bitcoin drops that block, Sequentia drops its own. The two chains reorganise together, so one confirmation policy covers both legs of the trade.

Anchoring also lets Sequentia see Bitcoin. Every node knows which Bitcoin blocks the chain has anchored, so it can check for itself that a given Bitcoin payment is in one of them. That check is what releases the asset to the buyer.

Go deeperWhy a Bitcoin reorganisation cannot cheat the seller

The asset is locked in an output that opens only for a spender who proves that a specific Bitcoin payment (to this address, of at least this amount) is in Bitcoin’s chain. Every node checks the proof against the chain’s own anchors.

If Bitcoin reorganises the payment away, the claim on Sequentia goes with it, and it cannot be mined again until the payment is back in Bitcoin’s chain. A buyer who spends the same bitcoin elsewhere never gets the asset, and the seller takes it back after the timeout.

The bitcoin side is an ordinary payment, with no hash lock and nothing special for a Bitcoin wallet to support. Details in Swaps and SeqDEX.

Try it

In the web wallet, open Swap, choose what you pay and what you get, Review, then Trade.

For charts, an order book and limit orders, use SeqDEX. It has no wallet of its own: it connects to yours, and your wallet asks you before anything is signed.
