Glossary

Simplifies the whitepaper, section iv glossary

Protocol terms

Anchor. The Bitcoin block that a Sequentia block points to: its header hash and its height, written inside the Sequentia block’s own header. See Bitcoin anchoring.

Asset id. The 256-bit number that identifies an asset. It is computed from the issuance transaction and the issuer’s declaration, so it cannot be forged or changed.

Block proposer (also leader, producer). The staker who wins the lottery for a block, builds it and submits it to the committee.

Blocksigner. A member of the committee for a given block.

Certificate. The proof that a quorum signed a block: one aggregated signature, a compact record of which members signed, and the seats they hold. About 300 bytes at any committee size.

Certified block. A valid block whose certificate reaches the quorum. Only a certified block is final.

Checkpoint. A record of a Sequentia block’s hash, written into a Bitcoin transaction. Once buried, nodes reject any fork below it. See Checkpoints in Bitcoin.

Collection window. The interval (6.75 seconds with a full committee) in which committee members gather proposals before signing.

Committee. The stakers entitled to sign the block at a given height: up to 250 seats, shared in proportion to stake.

Confidential transaction. A transfer that hides its amount and asset. Optional on Sequentia, and received at a visibly different address.

Controller and signer. The controller is the key that owns staked coins and the only key that can move them. The signer is the key that takes part in block production. They are normally the same; delegation separates them.

Covenant. An output whose spending conditions constrain what the spending transaction must itself pay. The building block of Sequentia’s contracts.

Delegation. Letting another key produce blocks on behalf of your stake, without giving it your coins.

Election score. The number a staker draws for a slot. The lowest wins the right to propose first.

Escaping stall. The rule that lets the chain continue with fewer signatures than a quorum when Bitcoin’s progress shows it has stalled. See Never stuck.

Finality modulo Bitcoin. A certified block is final against every competitor inside Sequentia and is undone only if Bitcoin undoes the block it is anchored to.

Finalized point. The highest certified block that has stood on a node’s chain for the observation window. The node rejects any fork at or below it.

Observation window. The three seconds a node waits after a block is certified, to be sure no competing certificate exists.

Participant (or staker). A validator with tokens at stake, eligible to be chosen as proposer or committee member.

Price list. A node’s list of the assets it accepts as fees, with a rate for each. Also called the fee whitelist.

Price server. A program that follows market data and publishes a price list for nodes to read.

Quorum. The minimum number of committee seats that must sign to certify a block: a strict majority, 126 of 250.

Reference unit. The abstract unit in which fees paid in different assets are compared. It is a unit of measure, not a token, and cannot be held. Named the reference fee atom in the implementation.

Registry. The public directory that maps asset ids to verified names, tickers and issuer domains.

Reissuance token. The key that lets its holder create more units of an asset.

Round. One attempt, of fixed length (9.45 seconds with a full committee), in which the committee backs one proposal and signs it.

Sidechain. A blockchain bound to Bitcoin. Sequentia is bound by anchoring, not by a peg.

Slot. The opportunity to produce the block at a given height.

Stake. Tokens locked to become eligible to produce blocks.

Sub-quorum block. A valid block accepted under the escaping-stall rule with fewer signatures than the quorum. Never final by itself, and loses to any certified block at the same height.

Supervised asset. An asset whose issuer declared, at issuance, the power to freeze coins at an address. See For issuers.

Unbonding output. Where a stake goes when withdrawn. It carries no weight and can be spent only after Bitcoin has advanced 2,016 blocks.

UTXO. Unspent transaction output: Bitcoin’s way of representing coins as individual outputs, not account balances. Sequentia uses the same model.

Validator. Anyone running a Sequentia full node, whether or not they hold any stake.

Second layers

Ark. A protocol in which an operator commits many users’ coins to one on-chain output, and users pay each other instantly off-chain while keeping the ability to leave on their own.

Batch. The shared on-chain output of an ark.

Leaf (or VTXO). One user’s coin inside an ark’s batch.

Lightning. A network of payment channels for instant payments.

HTLC. Hashed-timelock contract: a payment that can be claimed with a secret before a deadline or refunded after it. The basis of Lightning, and of swaps over Lightning.

Atomic swap. A trade whose two halves either both happen or neither does.
