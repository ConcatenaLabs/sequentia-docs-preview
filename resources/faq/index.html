FAQ

The basics

What is Sequentia, in one sentence?

A Bitcoin sidechain for every asset that is not bitcoin: stablecoins, stocks, bonds and commodities, held in your Bitcoin wallet, paying fees in any asset, and traded directly against real bitcoin. See What is Sequentia.

Is it live?

It runs as a public testnet, continuously since 5 July 2026. There is no mainnet yet. Everything on the testnet is free and has no value. See Status and roadmap.

What is the difference between Sequentia, the Sequence token, SEQ and tSEQ?

Sequentia is the network. The Sequence token is its staking token; its ticker is SEQ, and on the testnet tSEQ.

Who is behind it?

Sequentia is open source, developed at github.com/ConcatenaLabs. Its initial development is promoted by Concatena Labs, Inc. See Community.

Bitcoin

Is Sequentia an altcoin?

It is a separate blockchain with a staking token, so judge for yourself. What it does not do is offer a rival money. SEQ is not needed to pay fees, is not what anything is priced in, and is never inflated. The design goal is a world with one money, bitcoin, and a place for every other asset to live. See Why bitcoiners should care.

Is my bitcoin moved onto Sequentia?

No. Bitcoin stays on the Bitcoin blockchain, at your own address, under your own key. A Sequentia wallet is a Bitcoin wallet that also holds Sequentia assets, at the same address.

So there is no wrapped bitcoin?

Not in Sequentia’s design. Nothing in its consensus requires or favours a pegged bitcoin. On the testnet there is an optional wrapped asset, SBTC, for the two things bitcoin on its own chain cannot do on Sequentia: confidential transfers, and resting in a covenant such as a limit order. It is a trusted bridge, it is labelled as one, and you never need it to hold, send or swap bitcoin.

Why is it called a sidechain if there is no peg?

Because every Sequentia block is anchored to a Bitcoin block and is undone if Bitcoin undoes that block. The two chains are tied by reference, not by a deposit. See Bitcoin anchoring.

What happens if Bitcoin reorganises?

Sequentia reorganises with it. Every Sequentia block pointing at the dropped Bitcoin block is discarded, and its transactions are included again. This is deliberate: it is what keeps trades across the two chains consistent.

Is it a fork of Liquid?

It is a fork of Elements, the open-source codebase that Liquid also runs. Sequentia leaves Liquid’s federation and bitcoin peg switched off, and adds the open fee market, Bitcoin anchoring, proof of stake and Bitcoin’s address format. See Sequentia vs the rest.

Fees

Which asset pays my fees?

Any asset that block producers accept. Your wallet offers the accepted assets you hold, and shows the fee in that asset and in your currency before you sign. See The end of gas tokens.

Do I need SEQ?

No. SEQ has no special place in fees. You need it only if you want to stake.

What if I pay with an asset nobody accepts?

The transaction waits and is not confirmed. Replace it with one paying in a different asset. Wallets only offer assets their node accepts, so in normal use this does not come up.

Who decides the exchange rate?

Each block producer, for itself. Most read a price server that follows market data. There is no official rate and no rule of the network about what a fee is worth.

Using it

How long does a payment take?

An on-chain payment confirms in the next block, about a minute, and is final about ten seconds after that block is proposed. A payment between wallets over Ark arrives in seconds. A swap against on-chain bitcoin waits for Bitcoin blocks.

When is a payment final?

When its block is certified, it is final against anything inside Sequentia. It can be undone only if Bitcoin reorganises the block it is anchored to. Services that release funds elsewhere, such as a bridge, wait until that anchor is buried deep in Bitcoin. See Final in seconds.

Are my transactions private?

They are public by default, as on Bitcoin. Confidential transfers, which hide the amount and the asset, are available per transfer by receiving at a confidential address. For stronger privacy there is CoinJoin mixing in the web wallet and in Seqognito.

I lost my wallet.

  • Web wallet or Ambra: restore from your twelve-word recovery phrase.
  • Sequentia Core: restore the backup file.

Without the phrase or the backup nobody can recover it for you. On the testnet, make a new wallet and use the faucet again.

Can I issue my own asset?

Yes, in one transaction. See Issue an asset.

Assets and issuers

Can an issuer freeze my asset?

Only if the asset was issued as supervised, and that is public from the moment of issuance: you can check before accepting it. An issuer of a supervised asset can freeze the coins at an address. It can never spend them, and it cannot add this power to an asset later. Ordinary assets cannot be frozen by anyone. See For issuers.

How do I know an asset called “USDT” is the real one?

A wallet shows a name only when a registry has verified that the website named in the asset’s declaration publishes a file naming that exact asset. Without that proof the wallet shows the raw asset id.

Does Sequentia have smart contracts?

Yes, without a virtual machine. Contracts are covenants written in Bitcoin Script or Simplicity, whose cost is known before they run. Exchanges, loans, sales and regulated assets are built this way. See Contracts: Simplicity.

Staking and security

How are block producers chosen?

By a lottery among stakers, exactly proportional to stake. A committee of up to 250 stakers then signs each block. See Staking without inflation.

What do stakers earn?

The fees of the blocks they produce, in whatever assets users paid. No new SEQ is ever created.

Can stakers change the rules?

No. Every full node checks every rule itself and rejects an invalid block however many stakers signed it.

What if most stakers go offline?

The chain reads Bitcoin’s progress, recognises that it has stalled, and continues with fewer signatures until they return. See Never stuck.

Do I have to run a Bitcoin node to run a Sequentia node?

A Sequentia node needs to see Bitcoin to check anchors. On the testnet it uses a shared endpoint by default so it works at once; to verify without trusting anyone, point it at your own Bitcoin node. See Run a node.
